CrackWatch
Print the card

A printed card turns a phone photo into a crack gauge.

OpenCV 5 reads the card to get millimetres, measures the crack width with its uncertainty, and compares it with the last visit. An agent on Amazon Bedrock decides what happens next: a retake, a closer photo of one spot, or a ticket that only a human can approve.

Measured so far on 300 held-out synthetic photos: median-width error 0.17 mm, card found in 99.7 %. Real-photo validation is in progress and will be published here.

Guided inspection of a synthetic wall

Measure your own crack
  1. Print the A6 card at 100 % (actual size). Its 50 mm ruler must measure 50 mm.
  2. Tape it flat on the wall, 2 to 10 cm from the crack.
  3. Photograph card and crack together, facing the wall, without flash.

Your photo goes to a private storage bucket and is deleted automatically after 24 hours. Decoding drops EXIF and GPS data. Please do not photograph people.

    How it works

    1. Scale. cv2.aruco.CharucoDetector finds the card; findHomography (MAGSAC) maps the photo to the wall plane in millimetres.
    2. Quality. Blur is measured in pixels on the card's black-and-white edges; glare and tilt are checked before anything is measured.
    3. Width. A multi-scale Hessian ridge filter finds the crack; each cross-section is deconvolved with the blur measured on the card, giving sub-pixel widths and an uncertainty.
    4. Change. Both visits share the card's frame; if the card was re-glued, ORB + MAGSAC + ECC register the wall texture.
    5. Decision. Amazon Nova 2 Lite on Bedrock chooses one tool (retake, closer photo, compare, ticket, next visit). A deterministic policy validates every choice and takes over if the model fails or the daily budget is used.

    CrackWatch is a monitoring and triage aid, not a structural diagnosis. Thresholds are configurable defaults, not engineering norms. Tickets are never sent without a human decision.
